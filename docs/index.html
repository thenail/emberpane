How it works

Pick a layout. Everything falls into place.

Click Ember Pane's icon on the taskbar and choose how you'd like your screen divided. The apps you have open move into it, and new ones find a place by themselves.

See what is busy

A frame that shows the load

Every window gets a thin coloured frame. It turns from blue through amber to red as its app works harder, so the one that's making your fan spin stands out at a glance.

  • One full processor core reads as Busy, whatever size your computer is
  • Choose how quickly the colour follows short bursts of work
  • An app at rest keeps the border Windows gives it

Hands on

Resize and move by dragging

Rest the pointer in the gap between two windows and a line appears. Drag it, and the windows on both sides follow. Drag a window by its title bar and the areas light up: let go over one, and the window goes there.

  • Choose the gap between windows and the space at the screen edge
  • Rounded corners, slightly rounded, or as the app draws them
  • Moving works across monitors too

Stays out of the way

Nothing moves until you ask

Arranging starts off. Turn it on by picking a layout, and turn it off again whenever you like: every window stays where it is.

  • Leave any app where you put it, with its frame, or leave it alone entirely
  • Apps with a minimum size get the room they insist on
  • Each monitor and virtual desktop keeps its own layout

The small things

Made to stay out of your way

Free, and private by design

No cost, no account, no sign-up. It never connects to the network.

Every screen its own layout

Monitors and virtual desktops each keep theirs. Unplug a monitor and its layout is still there when you plug it back.

Small and quiet

Sits in the corner of your taskbar. The panel and Settings only run while they're open.

Speaks your language

Comes in 19 languages, from Danish to Japanese, and follows your Windows setting.

Easy to install

From the Microsoft Store, or a normal installer that needs no administrator password.

Colour per app

It's the app that's busy, so two windows of one app share a colour.

Questions

Good to know

Which versions of Windows are supported?

Windows 11, on regular PCs. Builds for ARM-based PCs are on the releases page, but haven't been tried on one yet.

What happens to Snap in Windows?

While Ember Pane is arranging, it turns Windows' own Snap off, because it handles dragging windows itself. Snap comes back as soon as arranging stops.

What if I have more windows than areas?

They share an area, in front of each other, and you switch between them as usual. New windows fit in by themselves.

Why won't some windows move?

Windows doesn't let an ordinary app move windows that run as administrator, so those stay where you put them. Some apps also have a minimum size: Ember Pane learns it and gives that window the room it insists on.

What do the colours measure?

How much processor time the app is using. One full processor core reads as Busy, whatever size your computer is, so the colours mean the same on a laptop as on a workstation. They're a guide, not a benchmark.

Is it finished?

This is an early version. It has been used on one monitor with one virtual desktop; more monitors, more virtual desktops and the translations are built but haven't been tried much yet. With the dark line or a wider frame, Ember Pane draws a click-through layer over your windows. If a video or game misbehaves, set the dark line to 0 in Settings, and please open an issue.

Can I use it without installing?

Yes. Each release also comes as a zip: extract it anywhere, open the EmberPane folder and run EmberPane.exe. Extract the whole zip first, as it won't run from inside it. Your settings are still kept in your Windows user profile, not in the folder.

How do I remove it?

Like any other app: open Settings › Apps › Installed apps, find Ember Pane and choose Uninstall. Running it from the zip? Quit Ember Pane from its Settings, turning off Start with Windows first if you turned it on, then delete the folder.

The fine print

Privacy

Ember Pane does not collect any data. It has no account, no analytics and no tracking, and it never connects to the network. Your settings are stored only on your computer, in your Windows user profile.

To colour the frames, Ember Pane reads how much processor time each app uses. That never leaves your computer. Its log names apps, never window titles, and stays on your computer too.
